# NordicBike AB — Returns Policy

This document covers the right of withdrawal for new purchases from NordicBike AB's online store. It is a distinct policy from warranty coverage (see policies/warranty.md): returns concern a customer's right to send back an unwanted item shortly after delivery, regardless of any defect, while warranty concerns coverage for defects that appear during ownership. Support conversations that mention wanting "money back" should be checked against which of these two policies actually applies — a customer asking for a refund because a bike has a defect months into ownership is a warranty/compensation matter (see policies/escalation.md for authority limits on compensation), not a returns matter, even though both involve money changing hands.

This distinction is worth stating plainly because the surface language customers use often does not distinguish the two situations for them — "I want my money back" can mean "I changed my mind about this purchase I received three days ago" (returns) or "This product broke after eight months and I think NordicBike owes me something for the inconvenience" (warranty plus, potentially, an escalation-triggering compensation demand). Reading past the surface request to the underlying situation — recency of delivery versus months or years of ownership, presence or absence of a stated defect — is the fastest way to route a case to the correct policy.

## Right of Withdrawal

Customers who purchase from NordicBike AB's online store have a **14-day right of withdrawal from the date of delivery**, in line with Swedish and EU distance-selling consumer protection law (distansavtalslagen / the EU Consumer Rights Directive). This right applies to online purchases of bikes, batteries, and accessories alike, and does not require the customer to give a reason for the return.

The 14-day period is calculated from the date the item is delivered to the customer, not from the date the order was placed. A withdrawal notice must be communicated to NordicBike within this window; the item itself does not need to be physically returned within the 14 days, provided the customer has clearly communicated their intent to withdraw and then returns the item within a reasonable additional period. This right exists independently of, and is not extended or shortened by, any product-specific warranty term — a bike purchased under the Winter 2024 Launch Promotion's extended warranty period (see policies/warranty.md Section 6), for example, still has an ordinary 14-day right of withdrawal like any other purchase, unrelated to its 36-month warranty term.

## Conditions

To qualify for a return under the right of withdrawal, the item must be:

- **Unused** — a bike that has been ridden extensively, or an accessory that shows signs of use beyond what is reasonably needed to inspect it, may not qualify for a full refund.
- **In its original packaging**, or packaging of equivalent protective quality, with all included accessories, manuals, and chargers.

Items that do not meet these conditions may still be eligible for a partial refund at NordicBike's discretion, reflecting any reduction in the item's value caused by handling beyond what is necessary to establish the nature and function of the item — consistent with the standard applied under Swedish distance-selling law. A short test ride around the block to confirm the bike functions as expected is generally treated as reasonable inspection use; regular commuting use over several days is generally not.

This policy governs new, unused purchases returned shortly after delivery. It does not apply to bikes, batteries, or accessories that have been in normal use for weeks, months, or years and are later found to have a defect — those situations are addressed by policies/warranty.md instead. A support agent who receives a request that sounds like a return but concerns an item that has clearly been in use for an extended period should redirect the conversation toward a warranty evaluation rather than processing it under this policy.

## Process

To initiate a return, the customer contacts NordicBike support (see company/support-contacts.md for channels) and states their intent to withdraw from the purchase within the 14-day window. NordicBike will provide return instructions and, where applicable, a return shipping label.

**Return shipping costs:**

- If the customer is returning an item simply because they changed their mind (no defect), the **customer pays return shipping**.
- If the item is defective, **NordicBike covers return shipping**, and the case should generally be handled as a warranty matter rather than a standard-return matter, since a defect discovered on an otherwise-wanted item is more accurately addressed through the warranty claim process described in policies/warranty.md Section 7.

Refunds for qualifying returns are issued to the original payment method once the returned item has been received and inspected. NordicBike aims to process the refund within a reasonable number of business days of receipt, consistent with statutory requirements under Swedish/EU distance-selling law. Refund amounts reflect any deduction applicable under the Conditions section above; a full, undiminished refund is issued where the item and packaging meet the unused-condition standard.

## How This Differs From a Warranty Claim

Because both a return and a warranty claim can end with money moving back to the customer, it is worth being explicit about how a support agent should tell the two apart in practice, since the customer's own wording rarely does this for them:

- **Timing relative to delivery.** A return is, by definition, something raised within 14 days of delivery. A request raised weeks, months, or years after delivery is not a returns-policy matter regardless of how it is phrased, and should be evaluated under policies/warranty.md instead.
- **Presence of a stated defect.** A customer who simply no longer wants an item they received in good working order is exercising the right of withdrawal. A customer reporting that something is broken, malfunctioning, or defective — even shortly after delivery — is more accurately describing a warranty situation, though a defect discovered very early in ownership may sometimes be handled as either a return or a warranty replacement depending on what the customer prefers and what is most practical.
- **What the customer is asking for.** "I want to send this back and get my money back" without any complaint about function points toward a return. "I want this fixed or replaced because it's broken" points toward a warranty claim. "I want compensation for the trouble this has caused me" points toward policies/escalation.md instead, since that is a compensation demand rather than either a return or a straightforward warranty repair.

When in doubt, asking the customer directly — "Did you want to send this back because you no longer want it, or is something wrong with it?" — resolves the ambiguity faster and more reliably than guessing from the surface language of the request.
