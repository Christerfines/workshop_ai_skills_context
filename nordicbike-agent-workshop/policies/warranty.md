# NordicBike AB — Warranty Policy

This document is the single authoritative source for all NordicBike AB warranty terms, exclusions, service bulletins, and legacy promotional clauses. Every warranty-related figure quoted elsewhere in the NordicBike knowledge base — in product pages, customer support conversations, or evaluation materials — is sourced from this document and must not be restated with different numbers. Where a product page or support conversation references a warranty term, treat this document as the ground truth and this document's section numbers as the citation to use.

This document is organized so that Sections 1–3 establish the three baseline coverage terms that apply by product type, Section 4 lists the exclusions that can disqualify an otherwise in-window claim, Sections 5 and 6 describe two specific overrides to the baseline terms (one narrower than the default, one broader), and Section 7 covers the mechanics of actually filing and processing a claim. A support agent evaluating any warranty case should expect to touch at least two of these sections for almost any real case: one of Sections 1–3 to establish the baseline term, and Section 4 to check whether an exclusion applies — and, for the two products these sections specifically concern, Section 5 (Aurora X3, specific batch) or Section 6 (Vinter Pro, specific purchase window) to check whether a more specific override changes the outcome.

## Section 1 — Standard Limited Warranty

NordicBike AB provides a Standard Limited Warranty of **24 months from the original purchase date** on whole-bike products: Aurora X3, Fjord Cargo, and Vinter Pro. This warranty covers manufacturing defects in materials and workmanship under normal use, including but not limited to defects in the frame, motor, drivetrain, electrical system, and structural components such as racks and mounting points.

Proof of purchase is required to make a claim under this warranty. See Section 7 for what qualifies as proof of purchase and how claims are processed.

The Standard Limited Warranty term of 24 months is the default term that applies to every whole-bike purchase unless a more specific provision elsewhere in this document overrides it for a particular product, batch, or purchase window. Two such overrides exist in this document: the Service Bulletin coverage described in Section 5, and the legacy promotional term described in Section 6. Both are narrower or more specific than this Section, and both must be checked before a final eligibility determination is made — an agent that applies only the 24-month standard term without checking Sections 5 and 6 for potential relevance risks reaching an incorrect conclusion in either direction (denying a claim that should be covered, or failing to apply an exclusion that should disqualify it).

A claim under this Section is evaluated in three steps: first, confirm the purchase date and product identity against proof of purchase or the customer record; second, compute the standard 24-month window from that date and compare it to today's date; third, and only if the claim is otherwise within scope, check Section 4 for any applicable exclusion and Sections 5–6 for any applicable product- or batch-specific override. A claim that is inside the standard window and not excluded, with no more specific override changing the outcome, is eligible for repair or replacement as described in Section 7.

Manufacturing defects covered under this Section include, without limitation: frame cracks or weld failures not caused by an accident; motor failure not caused by water ingress, unauthorized modification, or misuse; drivetrain component failure under normal use; electrical system faults, including display, wiring harness, and connector failures, not otherwise excluded under Section 4 or specifically addressed under Section 5; and failure of structural mounting points such as rack welds, kickstand mounts, or fender brackets.

The Standard Limited Warranty does not cover the items addressed separately in Section 2 (wear items) or Section 3 (standalone spare/replacement batteries), and it is subject in full to the exclusions enumerated in Section 4.

## Section 2 — Wear-Item Limited Coverage

Certain components are expected to wear out through normal use over a timeframe shorter than the bike's overall Standard Limited Warranty term, and are covered separately under a **6-month Wear-Item Limited Coverage from the original purchase date**. This coverage protects against manufacturing defects in these components during that shorter window; it does not extend normal wear-and-tear replacement beyond 6 months, and normal wear itself — even within the 6-month window — is not a manufacturing defect and is not covered.

Wear items covered under this Section:

- Tires
- Brake-pad and brake-disc friction material
- Brake and gear cables
- Grips
- Saddle

A wear item that fails due to a genuine manufacturing defect within 6 months of purchase is covered under this Section — for example, a tire that develops a sidewall failure from a manufacturing flaw in the casing, or a cable that snaps from a manufacturing defect in the cable strand itself, rather than from stretch and fraying accumulated through ordinary use. A wear item that simply wears out through normal use — brake pads worn thin from ordinary riding, tires worn from mileage, cables stretched from routine use — is not covered under this Section or any other Section of this warranty, regardless of how much time has elapsed since purchase, because ordinary wear is an expected consequence of use rather than a defect.

Distinguishing a manufacturing defect from ordinary wear in a wear item is often the central judgment call in a Section 2 claim, and it depends on the specific symptom described rather than the component category alone. A brake system that is "not as sharp as before" could reflect either ordinary pad wear (Section 2, and time-barred outside 6 months) or an underlying hydraulic or caliper defect that is not a wear item at all and falls under the 24-month Standard Limited Warranty in Section 1 instead. Where a customer's description does not clearly distinguish between these two possibilities, the correct response is to ask a clarifying question rather than default to either branch — see evaluation/adversarial-cases.md Archetype D for the general pattern this reflects.

## Section 3 — Spare/Replacement Battery Warranty

PowerPack 720 and PowerPack 900 batteries sold as **standalone spare or replacement parts** (i.e., not as the original integrated battery that shipped with a bike) carry their own warranty term of **12 months from the purchase date of the standalone battery**, independent of any bike's warranty status.

This term applies specifically to batteries purchased on their own — as a spare, or as a replacement for a battery that has already failed or degraded. It is distinct from, and shorter than, the 24-month Standard Limited Warranty that applies to a whole bike under Section 1, including the battery that is integrated into that bike at the time of original sale. In other words: an integrated battery that shipped with a new bike is covered under the bike's own Section 1 warranty for 24 months; a battery purchased separately afterward, whether as a spare or a replacement for a failed unit, is covered under this Section 3 for 12 months from its own purchase date, regardless of the age or warranty status of the bike it is used with.

This distinction matters because the 24-month figure is, by volume, the most common warranty figure a support agent will see across all case types, and it is tempting to apply it reflexively to any battery-related question. A standalone battery claim should always be checked specifically against this Section rather than against Section 1, and the purchase record should make clear whether the battery in question was purchased as part of a bike (Section 1 applies, 24 months) or on its own (Section 3 applies, 12 months). Coverage under this Section includes battery cells failing to hold a reasonable proportion of their rated capacity, charging circuitry failures, and connector or casing defects present from the standalone unit's own purchase date — it does not cover degradation caused by water damage, physical damage, or use with an incompatible charger.

Claims under this Section follow the same proof-of-purchase and claims-process requirements described in Section 7.

## Section 4 — Exclusions

The following exclusions apply across the Standard Limited Warranty (Section 1), Wear-Item Limited Coverage (Section 2), and Spare/Replacement Battery Warranty (Section 3), except where a more specific provision states otherwise:

**(i) Water/moisture damage.** Damage caused by water or moisture intrusion is excluded, including — explicitly — damage caused by high-pressure washing directed at or near the battery compartment or drivetrain electronics. NordicBike bikes are designed to tolerate normal rain and wet-road riding, including riding in typical Nordic weather conditions, but are not designed to withstand direct high-pressure spray at sealed electrical components, and damage from such exposure is a use-related exclusion rather than a manufacturing defect. This exclusion applies regardless of the customer's intent — accidental exposure and routine cleaning habits are treated the same way, since the warranty concerns the cause of the damage, not whether the customer meant to cause it.

**(ii) Unauthorized modification.** Unauthorized modification to the drivetrain or electrical system — including but not limited to installing aftermarket motors, controllers, batteries not sold by NordicBike, or software/firmware modifications — voids the Standard Limited Warranty **in its entirety for the remainder of the coverage period**. This is a full-warranty exclusion, not a partial or component-specific one: once an unauthorized drivetrain or electrical modification has been made, the bike's remaining Standard Limited Warranty term is void regardless of whether a subsequent issue is related to the modification itself. A frame crack unrelated to a motor swap, for instance, is still excluded once this clause has been triggered, because the exclusion voids the warranty in full rather than only the modified subsystem.

**(iii) Commercial or rental use.** Use of a NordicBike bike for commercial or rental purposes is excluded from the standard warranty terms in Sections 1–3, except on the Fjord Cargo Business Edition, which is a separate product tier (not covered by this document) specifically warranted for commercial use. This exclusion exists because commercial and rental use typically involves substantially higher mileage, load, and multiple-rider wear patterns than the standard warranty terms are calibrated for.

**(iv) Accident or misuse damage.** Damage resulting from accidents, crashes, improper assembly by the customer, or use outside the bike's intended purpose (for example, off-road use of a model not rated for it, or exceeding rated cargo capacity) is excluded.

**(v) Cosmetic wear.** Cosmetic wear such as paint scuffs, minor scratches, and fading from UV exposure that does not affect the bike's function is excluded.

These five exclusions are independent of one another — more than one can apply to a single claim, and any single applicable exclusion is sufficient to disqualify an otherwise in-window claim under Sections 1–3. None of these exclusions apply to claims under Section 5 or Section 6 differently than stated in this Section; Sections 5 and 6 describe when coverage applies, not additional exceptions to when it does not.

## Section 5 — Service Bulletin SB-2025-11 (Aurora X3 Battery-Connector Corrosion)

Service Bulletin SB-2025-11: Aurora X3 units in batch AX3-25A (manufactured 2025-01-01 through 2025-06-30) may exhibit battery-connector corrosion caused by a manufacturing sealant defect, presenting as intermittent power loss or failure to charge. This is covered under Section 1 as a manufacturing defect. This coverage does NOT apply if the corrosion's root cause is water ingress from customer-side high-pressure washing near the battery compartment (Section 4(i)) — visually similar corrosion can have either cause, and the root cause, not the visual symptom or batch membership, determines eligibility.

In practice, this means that batch membership (a serial number in the AX3-25A range) and a matching symptom (intermittent power loss, failure to charge) are **necessary but not sufficient** conditions for coverage under this Service Bulletin. A support agent evaluating a claim under SB-2025-11 must establish the root cause of the corrosion — through the customer's account of the bike's storage and washing habits, and where necessary through physical inspection at a service center — before determining eligibility. Two units from the same batch, presenting with the same symptom, can have opposite eligibility outcomes if their root causes differ: a unit with no water exposure and a genuine sealant defect is covered under this Section; a unit where the corrosion resulted from customer-directed high-pressure washing near the battery compartment falls under the Section 4(i) exclusion instead, and that exclusion overrides this Section's coverage.

Background on this bulletin, for context: a subset of Aurora X3 units manufactured in the first half of 2025 shipped with a battery-connector seal that, under laboratory review, was found to be prone to a manufacturing defect that allows minute moisture ingress over time even without any external water exposure beyond ordinary riding conditions. This is what Section 5 refers to as the "manufacturing sealant defect" — a defect present in the part itself, independent of how the customer used or cared for the bike. This is distinguishable in principle from corrosion caused by an external, customer-side event such as directing a high-pressure washer at or near the battery compartment, which forces water past seals not designed to withstand that kind of concentrated pressure regardless of whether the underlying seal also had the sealant defect. Because both causes can produce visually similar connector corrosion, a support agent should not treat visual inspection alone as conclusive — the customer's account of storage and cleaning habits is the primary signal available before a physical inspection, and it should be weighed directly rather than defaulted around.

## Section 6 — Legacy Promotion Grandfather Clause (Vinter Pro)

Winter 2024 Launch Promotion: Vinter Pro units purchased between 2024-06-01 and 2024-08-31 inclusive carry a 36-month warranty in place of the standard 24-month term in Section 1. All Vinter Pro purchases outside this window receive the standard 24-month term.

This promotional term is not printed on the Vinter Pro product page and is not mentioned in any customer-facing marketing material still in circulation; it exists only in this policy document. A support agent evaluating a Vinter Pro claim must check the customer's purchase date against this window before concluding that a claim outside the standard 24-month term is ineligible — a purchase date falling inside 2024-06-01 through 2024-08-31 inclusive extends coverage to 36 months from that purchase date, superseding the Section 1 default for that unit only. Purchases made before 2024-06-01 or after 2024-08-31 — including purchases in later years unrelated to the original 2024 promotion window — receive the ordinary Section 1 term with no extension.

Background on this clause, for context: the Winter 2024 Launch Promotion was a limited-time marketing offer run in the summer of 2024 to build early-season demand ahead of that year's winter riding season, and it extended the Vinter Pro's warranty term as an incentive for early adopters rather than discounting the purchase price. Because it was a time-limited promotion tied to a specific purchase window rather than an ongoing product feature, it is easy for both customers and support agents to lose track of it over time — a customer may remember vaguely that "there was some kind of promotion" without recalling the specific extended term, and an agent working from the Vinter Pro product page alone, which does not mention this clause at all, has no way to know the extended term exists without cross-referencing this Section. A purchase date is the only fact needed to resolve whether this clause applies; no other information about the customer's account of "a promotion" is required to check it, and a vague customer reference to "a launch deal" or "a promotion when I bought it" is sufficient reason to check the purchase date against this window even if the customer cannot recall the exact terms themselves.

## Section 7 — Claim Process & Proof of Purchase

**Proof of purchase** required to open any warranty claim under Sections 1, 2, or 3 consists of an order confirmation email or receipt showing the product purchased, the purchase date, and the price paid. A serial number, where applicable to the product, should also be confirmed against NordicBike's records as part of claims processing. Where a customer cannot supply proof of purchase from their own records, NordicBike's own purchase-history records (see customers/*.md-style internal customer records) should be checked first before asking the customer for additional documentation; if no matching purchase can be found in either place, the claim cannot proceed until product identity and purchase date are established by some other means — see evaluation/adversarial-cases.md Archetype D for the standard response pattern in this situation.

**Where claims are processed:** warranty claims may be opened and processed at any of NordicBike's three service centers — Stockholm, Gothenburg, or Malmö — regardless of where the bike was originally purchased or which service center is nearest to the customer's original point of sale. A customer is never required to use a specific service center based on their original point of purchase.

**Turnaround:** once a bike or battery has been received at a service center for inspection, repair turnaround time is governed by policies/shipping.md and is not restated here with different figures.

**Outcome of a successful claim:** a claim found eligible under Sections 1, 2, or 3, and not excluded under Section 4 (or, where applicable, covered under the more specific terms of Section 5 or Section 6), is resolved by repair or replacement of the defective component or unit at no cost to the customer, at NordicBike's discretion based on the nature of the defect. Cash refunds or compensation payments are a separate matter governed by policies/escalation.md's authority limits, not by this warranty policy — a customer requesting cash compensation in addition to, or instead of, a repair or replacement should have that request evaluated against policies/escalation.md's triggers rather than resolved as a warranty-repair matter.

## Summary: How to Evaluate Any Warranty Case

For a support agent applying this document to a real case, the following sequence covers the great majority of situations correctly:

1. **Confirm product identity and purchase date** from the customer's own account, cross-checked against the customer record and, where available, the serial number. Do not proceed on an assumed or guessed product, date, or serial — if any of these is missing or unclear, see evaluation/adversarial-cases.md Archetype D for the correct clarifying-question response.
2. **Determine which baseline term applies** — Section 1 (24 months, whole bike), Section 2 (6 months, wear item), or Section 3 (12 months, standalone battery) — based on what was purchased and whether it was purchased as part of a bike or on its own.
3. **Check Section 4 for exclusions.** Water/moisture damage, unauthorized modification, commercial/rental use, accident/misuse, and cosmetic wear can each independently disqualify an otherwise in-window claim. This step depends on establishing root cause, not just matching a visible symptom — see Section 5 for why this distinction matters even within a single, well-defined issue category.
4. **Check for a more specific override.** For an Aurora X3 in batch AX3-25A with a matching symptom, check Section 5. For a Vinter Pro, check Section 6 regardless of whether the customer mentions a promotion by name — a vague reference to "a deal" or "a launch promotion" is enough reason to check.
5. **Check for an escalation trigger** per policies/escalation.md before finalizing a response — a case can be warranty-eligible and still require escalation for a separate reason (a compensation demand, a legal threat, an out-of-catalog request) that exists alongside, not instead of, the underlying warranty determination.
6. **State the outcome explicitly**, cite the specific section(s) the determination rests on, and communicate next steps (repair or replacement process, shipping arrangement per policies/shipping.md, or a clarifying question if the case cannot yet be resolved).

This sequence is also the basis for the 6-item quality gate defined in evaluation/scoring-rubric.md, which checks that each of these steps was actually performed rather than skipped in the interest of a shorter, cheaper response.
