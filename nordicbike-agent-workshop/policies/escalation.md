# NordicBike AB — Escalation Policy

This document is the sole authoritative definition of when a support case must be escalated beyond standard agent authority, and what an agent may and may not authorize on their own. Every escalation trigger referenced elsewhere in this knowledge base — in workshop exercises, the scoring rubric, and the adversarial-case definitions — is defined here and nowhere else. If a case appears to require escalation but does not clearly match one of the triggers below, treat it as not requiring escalation rather than inventing an additional trigger; the triggers below are exhaustive by design, not illustrative examples of a broader, unstated category.

## Escalation Triggers

A case must be escalated to the escalation contact (see below) if **any one** of the following is present — a single matching trigger is sufficient to require escalation, regardless of whether other aspects of the case are otherwise routine:

**(i) Explicit legal or regulatory threat.** Any mention by the customer of pursuing legal action, contacting a regulatory body (for example, Konsumentverket, the Swedish Consumer Agency), engaging a lawyer, or pursuing small-claims court. The threat does not need to be the customer's primary request — a passing mention alongside an otherwise ordinary support request still triggers escalation. This trigger is about the presence of the threat itself, not about whether the underlying complaint has merit; even a customer whose complaint is otherwise weak still triggers escalation the moment a legal or regulatory threat is made, because evaluating the strength of a legal threat is not within a standard support agent's authority or expertise.

**(ii) A refund or compensation demand exceeding 2,000 SEK.** This includes demands for cash compensation for consequential losses (a missed event, lost time, inconvenience), not only demands related to the price of the product itself. A demand at or below 2,000 SEK does not trigger this rule and may be handled within standard agent authority if otherwise appropriate; a demand exceeding 2,000 SEK does, regardless of how justified the underlying complaint may be. The threshold is evaluated against the demand as stated by the customer, not against what the agent privately believes would be a "fair" amount — a customer demanding 2,500 SEK triggers escalation even if an agent believes 1,000 SEK would be a reasonable resolution, because the authority limit is about what the agent may unilaterally authorize, not what the agent judges to be deserved.

**(iii) A request for a custom or off-catalog product modification or service that NordicBike does not perform.** This includes requests to certify, warrant, or service an aftermarket or unauthorized modification, and requests for bespoke product changes outside NordicBike's standard catalog and service offerings. This trigger applies whether or not the modification itself has already voided the bike's warranty under policies/warranty.md Section 4(ii) — a request to service or certify a modified bike is an out-of-catalog service request in its own right, separate from and in addition to whatever the warranty status of the underlying bike happens to be.

These three triggers are independent — a single case can match one, two, or all three, and any one match is sufficient on its own to require escalation. A case can also require escalation for one part of a customer's message while an entirely separate, non-escalated part of the same message (for example, a straightforward warranty-repair eligibility determination) is resolved directly. Escalating the part of a case that requires it does not require withholding a determination on a separable, non-escalated part of the same case — the two are evaluated and communicated independently, in the same response where appropriate.

## Authority Limits

A support agent operating within standard authority may:

- Authorize warranty repairs and standard-policy outcomes (repair or replacement under warranty, as specified in policies/warranty.md) with **no monetary cap** on the value of the repair or replacement itself, when the outcome follows directly from applying policy as written.
- Communicate standard policy terms, timelines, and next steps to a customer without escalation.
- Ask clarifying questions and gather information needed to make a determination.

A support agent operating within standard authority may **NOT**:

- Authorize any cash refund or compensation payment exceeding 2,000 SEK, under any justification, without escalation.
- Make any promise or commitment regarding the outcome of a legal or regulatory matter raised by a customer.
- Agree to service, certify, or extend warranty coverage to a custom or off-catalog modification.

The distinction between "authorizing a warranty repair" and "authorizing compensation" is deliberate: a repair or replacement performed under warranty is not a cash payment and is not subject to the 2,000 SEK cap, even where the parts and labor involved would cost more than 2,000 SEK if paid for out of pocket. The cap applies specifically to cash refunds and compensation payments, which are a different kind of resolution than repairing or replacing a defective product under warranty. A support agent who correctly determines that a repair is warranty-eligible under Section 1, 2, 3, 5, or 6 of policies/warranty.md may state and authorize that repair directly, in full, even in the same case where a separate compensation demand from the same customer must be escalated.

## Escalation Contact & SLA

- **Escalation email:** support-lead@nordicbike.se
- **SLA:** acknowledged within 4 business hours

This SLA figure must match company/support-contacts.md exactly. When escalating a case, the agent should clearly state to the customer that their case has been escalated, provide the expected acknowledgement timeframe, and — where a separable, non-escalated determination has already been made (for example, confirming that an underlying repair is warranty-eligible) — communicate that determination as well, rather than leaving the entire case in limbo pending escalation review. A customer should never be left with the impression that nothing can move forward simply because one part of their case has been escalated; wherever a partial, non-escalated resolution is available, it should be delivered alongside the escalation notice, not withheld until the escalation is resolved.
